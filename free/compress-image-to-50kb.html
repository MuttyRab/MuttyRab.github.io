How it works

Your browser redraws the photo and tries lower JPEG qualities (and, if needed, smaller dimensions) until the file is under 50 KB, keeping the best quality that fits. It works offline once the page has loaded.

Questions

Why does my photo need to be under 50 KB?

Upload forms often set a maximum file size. Small limits (about 10–50 KB) are common for signatures and ID photos on exam and government portals; application and profile photos are often allowed 100–500 KB. Check the form’s own instructions for the exact limit and pixel size.

Will the photo lose quality?

Very large photos are first scaled to 2,400 pixels on the long side. Then it keeps the highest JPEG quality that still fits under 50 KB, and makes the picture smaller (fewer pixels) only if quality alone isn’t enough. At 50 KB expect a small, slightly soft image — fine for a form, not for printing.

Can I compress a PNG or a screenshot?

Yes. A compressed result is a JPG, because JPG is far smaller for photos (transparent areas become white). A file that is already under the limit is left exactly as it is.
